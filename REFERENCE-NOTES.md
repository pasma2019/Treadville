# TREADVILLE --- SIGNATURE VISUAL REFERENCE NOTES

## Creative Direction Master Reference for OpenCode

**Purpose:** This document is the visual source of truth for the current
Treadville Premium Visual Redesign.

**Primary reference image:**
`public/design-reference/treadville-signature-reference.png`

**Status:** Prototype / stakeholder presentation direction

**Audience:** OpenCode implementation agent, Pascal / Pasco Labs

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 00 --- NON-NEGOTIABLE CREATIVE NORTH STAR

The supplied reference image is not a loose inspiration.

It is the **primary visual north star** for the redesign.

The objective is not to make Treadville "look nicer."

The objective is to make the website feel like it was art-directed by a
**top-tier international brand and digital experience agency**.

The finished experience should communicate:

-   premium
-   cinematic
-   editorial
-   confident
-   tactile
-   sophisticated
-   modern
-   African in origin without becoming stereotypical
-   globally credible
-   commercially desirable
-   intentionally designed

The user must be able to open the homepage and immediately feel:

> "This is a serious premium brand."

The website should create a visual reaction before the user has read
every word.

The reference should therefore guide:

-   composition
-   product scale
-   lighting
-   image treatment
-   colour
-   gradient behaviour
-   glass material
-   typography
-   spacing
-   hierarchy
-   transitions
-   visual rhythm
-   perceived quality

Do not reduce the reference to "dark brown + coffee."

The deeper design language is:

**cinematic imagery + sophisticated colour atmosphere + editorial
typography + restrained glass + intentional negative space + premium
product art direction.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 01 --- WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

Treadville should no longer feel like a conventional local business
website.

It should feel like a **premium agricultural and specialty-food brand
experience**.

The homepage should behave almost like a short visual brand film.

The user moves through distinct visual worlds:

1.  Coffee
2.  Tea
3.  Horticulture
4.  Grains

These are not four ordinary category cards.

They are four **visual chapters of one brand universe**.

Each chapter should have:

-   its own colour atmosphere
-   its own hero imagery
-   its own lighting character
-   its own product story
-   its own visual identity
-   the same Treadville typography and interaction language

The chapters must feel different without looking like four unrelated
websites.

# 02 --- REFERENCE IMAGE ANALYSIS PRINCIPLES

When comparing the implementation against the reference, evaluate these
dimensions in order:

## A. First impression

Does the opening frame immediately look expensive?

## B. Composition

Is every major object intentionally positioned?

## C. Product scale

Does the product have breathing room?

## D. Image quality

Does the imagery resemble professional advertising photography rather
than generic stock imagery or illustration?

## E. Lighting

Does light create depth, materiality and emotion?

## F. Colour

Does colour create atmosphere rather than merely decorate the page?

## G. Typography

Does the typography feel editorial and authoritative?

## H. Glass

Does glass feel like premium physical material rather than a UI trend?

## I. Negative space

Does empty space feel deliberate and luxurious rather than unfinished?

## J. Motion

Does movement feel slow, controlled and cinematic?

# 03 --- HERO COMPOSITION

The Hero is the most important part of the website.

The first viewport is the user's first judgment of the brand.

The Hero should not look like:

``` text
TEXT                         EMPTY SPACE
```

It should feel like:

``` text
EDITORIAL TYPE
       +
CINEMATIC PRODUCT ART
       +
ATMOSPHERIC LIGHT
       +
GLASS INFORMATION
       +
CONTROLLED NEGATIVE SPACE
```

The composition should have a clear focal point.

The product should generally occupy approximately **25--35% of the
visual field**, depending on the image.

Avoid enormous products that dominate the entire frame.

Luxury advertising frequently gains perceived quality from restraint.

The product must be:

-   large enough to read
-   small enough to breathe
-   positioned intentionally
-   separated from the headline
-   integrated into the lighting
-   visually grounded
-   free of awkward clipping

# 04 --- COFFEE VISUAL WORLD

## Emotional direction

Coffee should feel:

-   powerful
-   warm
-   refined
-   sensual
-   sophisticated
-   bold
-   tactile

## Preferred visual ingredients

Use combinations of:

-   premium coffee packaging
-   espresso cup
-   dark ceramic
-   roasted beans
-   coffee grounds
-   subtle steam
-   controlled reflections
-   rich shadows
-   cinematic highlights
-   subtle copper illumination
-   warm amber light
-   dark espresso surfaces

The composition should feel suitable for a global coffee advertising
campaign.

## Avoid

Do NOT default to:

-   coffee plantations
-   generic farm panoramas
-   smiling farmers
-   tourist imagery
-   cliché African imagery
-   giant upright coffee bags
-   cartoon coffee illustrations
-   line-art as the primary hero visual
-   excessive props
-   clutter
-   cheap stock photography

The coffee product should look desirable enough that a user wants to
reach through the screen and pick it up.

# 10 --- GRADIENT STRATEGY

Gradients are essential, but they must look like **cinematic
illumination**.

Do not create obvious blobs.

Do not use generic SaaS gradients.

Do not make the gradient itself the hero.

Instead use layered lighting:

1.  base environmental colour
2.  radial light source
3.  secondary atmospheric glow
4.  directional vignette
5.  subtle colour spill
6.  shadow
7.  product highlights

The viewer should feel:

> "There is light in this scene."

Not:

> "Someone added a CSS gradient."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11 --- GLASSMORPHISM

Glassmorphism is part of the premium language, but it must be
restrained.

Use glass where it creates hierarchy.

Best uses:

-   floating navigation
-   hero information panel
-   slide controls
-   important filters
-   cart drawer
-   selected interface states

Glass should feel:

-   translucent
-   thin
-   refined
-   expensive
-   integrated with the background
-   slightly luminous at edges

Preferred characteristics:

-   low-to-medium opacity
-   subtle blur
-   hairline border
-   restrained shadow
-   subtle highlight
-   visible background atmosphere

Avoid:

-   glass everywhere
-   huge opaque panels
-   excessive blur
-   neon borders
-   "frosted card grid" aesthetics
-   generic dashboard glassmorphism

The glass should support the photography, not hide it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12 --- TYPOGRAPHY

Typography is one of the strongest existing Treadville assets.

Preserve the editorial character.

The visual reference should influence:

-   scale
-   line length
-   hierarchy
-   spacing
-   alignment
-   contrast

Hero headlines should feel:

-   large
-   confident
-   editorial
-   slightly dramatic
-   deliberate

Do not fill the screen with text.

Do not make paragraphs compete with the imagery.

Use concise supporting copy.

Small uppercase metadata can provide the premium editorial language:

-   origin
-   category
-   grade
-   process
-   chapter number
-   provenance

But metadata must remain secondary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13 --- PRODUCT ART DIRECTION

Every product image should be treated as advertising photography.

A product shot should have:

-   a hero object
-   controlled lighting
-   believable material
-   intentional shadows
-   subtle depth
-   clear focal hierarchy
-   breathing room

The product must not appear pasted onto a gradient.

The background and product lighting must feel like one scene.

For coffee packaging specifically:

-   avoid excessive vertical stretching
-   avoid oversized bags
-   avoid distorted labels
-   avoid impossible geometry
-   avoid random branding text that could be mistaken for official
    product copy

If the real Treadville package asset is available, use it.

If it is not available, use clearly documented prototype imagery and
keep the architecture easy to swap.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14 --- PHOTOGRAPHY VS GENERATED PROTOTYPE IMAGERY

The absence of real Treadville photography is a known prototype
limitation.

Do not hide that limitation.

Generated imagery may be used to establish the visual direction.

However:

**Generated imagery must never be represented as genuine Treadville
photography.**

Do not claim:

-   a generated farm is Treadville's farm
-   a generated worker is a Treadville employee
-   a generated facility is a Treadville facility
-   a generated package is the final approved product packaging
-   a generated landscape is an exact Treadville location

Use generated imagery as **creative prototype art**.

When real photography arrives, the visual system should allow the asset
to be replaced without rebuilding the page.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15 --- IMAGE PROMPT QUALITY STANDARD

If an image-generation workflow is used, prompts should describe:

-   camera perspective
-   focal length feel
-   lighting direction
-   material
-   surface
-   depth of field
-   composition
-   colour atmosphere
-   negative space
-   product placement
-   visual hierarchy

Do NOT use vague prompts such as:

> "premium coffee image"

Use art-direction language such as:

> "Luxury editorial product campaign, premium matte coffee package
> positioned slightly right of center, dark ceramic espresso cup in
> foreground, scattered roasted beans, controlled copper rim light, deep
> espresso-black background, subtle amber atmospheric glow, cinematic
> shadow falloff, shallow depth of field, tactile packaging texture,
> restrained composition, substantial negative space for editorial
> typography, sophisticated international food-brand advertising
> aesthetic."

The exact prompt can vary by chapter, but the quality standard must
remain high.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16 --- HERO GLASS PANEL

The floating hero panel should not behave like a normal card.

It should feel like a piece of premium interface hardware floating over
the campaign.

Recommended hierarchy:

-   chapter number
-   category
-   short editorial descriptor
-   2--4 verified facts
-   primary CTA
-   secondary action where appropriate

The panel must not cover the hero subject unnecessarily.

It should interact with the image.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17 --- NAVIGATION

The navigation should feel like part of the visual identity.

Keep it:

-   minimal
-   confident
-   compact
-   translucent where appropriate
-   highly legible

Avoid a large conventional navigation bar.

The navigation should not compete with the hero.

On mobile, the menu should feel like a continuation of the same material
system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18 --- MOTION

Motion should feel expensive.

Preferred:

-   slow crossfade
-   subtle scale
-   restrained parallax
-   gentle opacity transitions
-   controlled panel movement
-   deliberate hover states

Avoid:

-   bouncing
-   excessive spring animations
-   fast card animations
-   constant movement
-   gimmicky transitions

Motion should be noticed emotionally, not mechanically.

The user should feel the page moving rather than watching an animation
demo.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19 --- NEGATIVE SPACE

Negative space is a feature.

Do not fill every empty area.

But distinguish:

### Intentional negative space

-   gives product room
-   improves hierarchy
-   increases luxury
-   creates visual tension

from:

### Accidental emptiness

-   missing imagery
-   underdeveloped composition
-   oversized unused columns
-   content pushed too far away

The test:

> If the empty area disappeared, would the composition become worse?

If yes, preserve it.

If no, the space may need refinement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20 --- DISCOVERY SECTION

The Discovery section should continue the visual story after the Hero.

The four categories should not suddenly become generic cards.

Use:

-   editorial composition
-   strong category imagery
-   atmospheric colour
-   controlled typography
-   subtle glass where justified
-   asymmetric layout
-   different image scales

The section should feel like:

**"Enter another world."**

not:

**"Choose a category."**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21 --- FEATURED PRODUCTS

Featured products must feel desirable.

The visual hierarchy should clearly distinguish:

-   lead product
-   supporting products
-   category
-   product identity
-   price / availability where appropriate

Avoid making every product card visually identical.

Use scale and composition to establish a lead product.

If real product photography is unavailable, use sophisticated prototype
product art rather than empty gradient blocks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22 --- PROVENANCE

Provenance should retain its editorial/data character.

The visual reference does not mean every section must become dark.

Use contrast deliberately.

The site should move between:

-   cinematic dark
-   warm ivory
-   botanical green
-   graphite
-   champagne
-   controlled atmospheric surfaces

This creates rhythm.

Do not flatten everything into one background colour.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 23 --- STORY SECTION

Story needs visual rest, but not visual emptiness.

Use one strong visual element where appropriate:

-   premium image
-   editorial quote
-   oversized statistic
-   crop of product photography
-   tactile material detail

Do not create a collage.

One powerful element is better than five mediocre ones.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Use authenticity through:

-   origin

Avoid relying on:

-   flags everywhere

-   generic African patterns

**Kenyan origin, international confidence.**

# 25 --- RELATIONSHIP TO TREADVILLE'S REAL BUSINESS

The current Treadville website positions the company around premium
Kenyan specialty coffee, Kirinyaga/Mt. Kenya origin, quality,
traceability and global markets. The redesign should elevate that
proposition visually rather than contradict it.

Existing verified brand/content themes include:

-   premium Kenyan Arabica
-   Kirinyaga / Mt. Kenya origin
-   specialty quality
-   80+ SCA positioning
-   washed and anaerobic processing
-   traceability
-   export capability
-   private-label capability
-   long industry experience

Do not invent new certifications, awards, facilities, customers or
statistics.

The visual ambition can be extremely high while the factual content
remains disciplined.

# 26 --- DESIGN SYSTEM RULE

Do not create one-off visual hacks for individual sections when the
underlying design language can be expressed through reusable
tokens/components.

Prefer:

-   shared atmosphere tokens
-   shared glass materials
-   shared image treatment
-   shared chapter metadata
-   shared typography
-   shared spacing
-   reusable visual utilities

But do not refactor working architecture merely for theoretical
cleanliness.

Visual quality comes first within the existing architecture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27 --- WHAT "PREMIUM" DOES NOT MEAN

Premium does NOT mean:

-   more gradients
-   more shadows
-   more glass
-   more animations
-   bigger typography
-   more gold
-   more decoration
-   more text
-   more cards

Premium means:

**better decisions.**

Every visual element must earn its place.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28 --- WHAT "CINEMATIC" DOES NOT MEAN

Cinematic does NOT mean:

-   dark everything
-   giant glowing circles
-   fake film grain everywhere
-   excessive blur
-   dramatic effects for their own sake

Cinematic means:

-   controlled light
-   depth
-   atmosphere
-   focal hierarchy
-   composition
-   pacing
-   contrast
-   emotional visual storytelling

# 29 --- QUALITY BAR

Before declaring the visual work complete, compare the rendered site
against the reference image at:

-   1280 × 720
-   1280 × 800
-   1280 × 900
-   390 × 844
-   430 × 932

At desktop ask:

> "Could this opening frame plausibly belong to a serious international
> premium food/agricultural brand?"

At mobile ask:

> "Does the experience still feel intentionally art-directed rather than
> merely responsive?"

At both ask:

> "Is the product imagery doing enough visual work?"

If the answer is no, do not hide behind passing technical checks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 30 --- VISUAL DEFECT PRIORITY

When deciding what to fix, prioritize in this order:

### P0 --- Brand perception blockers

-   weak hero imagery
-   giant/awkward product scale
-   empty hero composition
-   generic-looking imagery
-   poor visual hierarchy

### P1 --- Premium perception issues

-   weak gradients
-   flat lighting
-   cheap-looking glass
-   inconsistent product treatment
-   weak section transitions

### P2 --- Polish

-   spacing
-   minor typography adjustments
-   subtle border treatment
-   small responsive refinements

Do not spend an hour polishing a 1px border while the hero image still
looks like a placeholder.

-   old implementation

-   generic design convention

# 32 --- NO GENERIC AI LOOK

The final result must avoid the common "AI-generated website" signals:

-   excessive gradients
-   identical glass cards
-   symmetrical sections everywhere
-   huge generic headings
-   random floating blobs
-   decorative shapes with no purpose
-   generic stock imagery
-   fake statistics
-   repetitive cards
-   excessive rounded corners
-   predictable Tailwind layouts
-   everything centered
-   everything in containers of the same width

Instead use:

-   asymmetric composition
-   intentional cropping
-   strong focal objects
-   controlled whitespace
-   editorial rhythm
-   custom visual treatment
-   disciplined colour
-   sophisticated typography
-   visual tension

# 33 --- TECHNICAL TRANSLATION

The reference should ultimately be expressed through:

-   reusable image components
-   responsive image positioning
-   CSS variables/tokens
-   controlled gradients
-   glass materials
-   accessible controls
-   progressive enhancement

-   reduced-motion support

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 34 --- IMAGE SWAP ARCHITECTURE

Prototype imagery must be replaceable.

Ideal architecture:

``` text
visual identity
      ↓
image asset
      ↓
chapter configuration
      ↓
responsive presentation
```

When Treadville provides final photography, the agent should be able to
replace the source asset without rebuilding the Hero architecture.

The same principle should apply to Discovery and Featured.

# 36 --- FINAL ACCEPTANCE TEST

Do not ask:

> "Did we implement the requested components?"

Ask:

> "Would Pascal confidently open this website in front of Eunice?"

Then ask:

> "Would Eunice immediately perceive a significant improvement over the
> current Treadville website?"

Then ask:

> "Does the visual quality justify the phrase 'premium'?"

Then ask:

> "Does the site feel like Pasco Labs delivered an agency-level
> experience rather than an AI-generated template?"

The answer to all four should be:

**YES.**

If not, identify the highest-impact visual weakness and correct it
before declaring completion.

**quality before quantity.**

**confidence before explanation.**
